# Naming an enemy in the Lovecraftian card-game style

How to make an enemy name that sounds like it belongs beside Arkham Horror: The Card Game's encounter cards and Lovecraft's stories, by a written method instead of taste. The counts come from a survey of the card game's 649 enemies and the names in Lovecraft's 105 fiction texts. The patterns in § 1 and § 2 also work outside horror: swap the word stock.

## 1. Decide what the name must say

Answer three things from the enemy sheet before choosing any word:

1. **One or many?** A common enemy that appears in copies gets a **role name** (what kind of man or thing). Only a unique boss or villain gets a **person's name** or a **"The X" title**. In the card game, 2 of 405 non-unique enemies carry a person's name; 125 of 244 uniques do.
2. **Man or monster?** Humans get flat job words; monsters get dread words (§ 3). Mixing them makes the name lie about what the card is.
3. **Which family?** If the enemy belongs to an outfit (a gang, a cult), its name shares the outfit's word with its mates (pattern B).

## 2. Patterns

Pick the pattern, then fill it from the word stock. Count = how many card-game enemies use it.

| Pattern | Shape | Count | Tells the player | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Behaviour + kind | *-ing* word or adjective + noun: Hunting Nightgaunt, Ravenous Ghoul, Grappling Horror, Hired Gun | 161 | what it will do to you | monsters first; humans only when the first word is a trade word (Hired) |
| B. Outfit + job | shared outfit word + job noun: Gang Enforcer / Gang Soldier; Coterie Agent / Assassin; Lodge Enforcer / Jailor | 147 | which side, then which job | human commons in a crew; a monster family (Deep One Bull, Mi-Go Guard) |
| C. Job of X | noun + of/from/in + place or master: Priest of Dagon, Keeper of Secrets, Lurker in the Dark | 97 | whom it serves or where it lurks | cultists and lurking monsters |
| D. One plain word | Acolyte, Zealot, Mobster, Wraith | 21 | a type everyone knows | only when the word isn't already a famous name |
| E. Boss's possessive | O'Bannion's Thug, Dagon's Brood | 8 | whose man it is | soldiers of a named boss (the boss's name comes first) |
| F. Compound agent | Flesh-Eater, Corpse-Taker, Web-Spinner, night-gaunt, moon-beast | 10 + Lovecraft's kinds | what it eats, takes or does | monsters and creature kinds |
| G. "The X" title | The Red-Gloved Man, The Bloodless Man, The Thing That Follows; Lovecraft's the Haunter of the Dark | 35 | one of a kind; a rumour | unique villains and bosses |
| H. Person (+ subtitle) | Naomi O'Bannion, Runs This Town; Lovecraft's Old Whateley, Granny Orne | 127 | someone with a past | unique human bosses and villains only |
| I. Alien proper name | Umôrdhoth, Nyarlathotep, Ghatanothoa | 39 | not of this world, not to be beaten | old gods and their parts, never humans |

Rules that come with the patterns:

- **Two words is the norm** (283 of 444 role names). One word only for pattern D; three only when the outfit is two words (Clover Club Bouncer).
- **First word = threat, outfit or trade; last word = the job or kind.** The last word must state the role on its own.
- **A crew shares its first word** (Coterie ×9, Mi-Go ×9, Deep One ×6, Gang ×3, Lodge ×3). Two enemies of one outfit read as one gang only if they share it. A shared word is not the place for a pun.
- **Say it straight.** The card game's crime names are period slang without jokes (Hired Gun, Mob Enforcer, Mobster). Wit belongs in a unique villain's subtitle, never in a common's name.
- **Cult names come from devotion** (Acolyte, Disciple, Initiate, Zealot, Priest of X); **crime names from employment** (Enforcer, Bouncer, Guard, Thug, Informant). Don't give a gangster a cult word or the reverse.
- **Places feed names** (Innsmouth Troublemaker, Spider of Leng). If your setting's cities and outfits aren't named yet, use a common noun (the card game's own Gang, Mob, Lodge are common nouns) and never invent a proper name the project owner hasn't approved.

## 3. Word stock

The card game sits in the 1920s. Match your own period: take the shape, swap the words, and drop words from eras or themes your game keeps out (e.g. Prohibition words in a 1950s game, war words if the war stays offscreen).

- **Crime jobs, 1930s to 50s slang:** enforcer, muscle, heavy, goon, hood, torpedo, trigger, button man, hired gun, bruiser, slugger, palooka, pug, bouncer, collector, lookout, wheelman, fixer, shakedown man.
- **Waterfront words** (*On the Waterfront*, 1954; Lovecraft's Red Hook): dock, pier, wharf, quay, slip, hook (the longshoreman's cargo hook), shape-up (the morning hiring line), gang (a dock work crew), cargo, bale, winch, piling, bollard, tide, brine, harbor, longshoreman, stevedore.
- **Monster first words** (most used in the card game): hunting, vengeful, ravenous, frenzied, grasping, grappling, screeching, slithering, stalking, crawling, lurking, drowned, hollow.
- **Monster last words** (card-game heads and Lovecraft's nouns, by number of stories they appear in): thing 99, horror 75, dweller 18, watcher 18; hybrid, brood, spawn, beast, thrall, terror, swarm, hound, harbinger, haunter, lurker, whisperer, shambler, eater, gaunt.
- **Lovecraft people:** old New England surnames (Whateley, Marsh, Gilman, Waite, Orne, Curwen, Tillinghast, Peaslee, Akeley, Pickman), town titles before a surname (Old, Wizard, Granny, Squire, Goodman), plain city names for working men and crooks (Angelo Ricci, Joe Czanek, Manuel Silva). Take the *kind* of name, never these names.
- **Lovecraft beings:** hard consonant clusters and hyphens (th, gh, hl, ggu; Rhan-Tegoth, Gnoph-keh) for gods; one-syllable coinages (gug, zoog, ghast, wamp) or plain compounds (night-gaunt, moon-beast, toad-thing) for kinds.

## 4. Localized names

If the game ships in other languages:

- Decide once whether names are transliterated or translated, and keep it for every enemy. Transliteration usually keeps the pulp flavour; meaning-translated names tend to sound invented.
- Check each word's local spelling and reading on its own. A word that reads as a common local word, a famous brand or another loanword misleads (in one Korean pass, "Hook" read as the boxing punch and "Harbor" as Harvard).
- Keep a length limit in the target script so names fit the card.
- First and last sounds must not echo the current roster or the heroes. A shared outfit word is the one allowed repeat, and only inside the outfit.

## 5. What not to use

- **Any actual card-game enemy name** (Hired Gun, Gang Enforcer, Mob Enforcer, Mobster, O'Bannion's Thug, Clover Club Bouncer and the rest), or its outfit words: Coterie, Silver Twilight, Lantern Club, Clover Club, O'Bannion, Brotherhood, Esoteric Order of Dagon. The shape is free; the name is not.
- **Any Lovecraft proper name verbatim** (people, beings, places) and the big-name kinds (Deep One, shoggoth, ghoul, Elder Thing, Great Old One, Mi-Go), unless the project owner has explicitly chosen to use one.
- **Racist or xenophobic sources.** Lovecraft's Red Hook gives a setting, not its view of immigrants; the boxer nickname in *Herbert West: Reanimator* is a slur. No ethnic tags in an enemy's name.
- **Endings the heroes own** (e.g. if heroes are "-Man", no enemy is), and for an unkillable god, any name that sounds beatable.
- **Puns and double readings** in a common's name. The card game never does it.

## 6. Procedure

1. Answer § 1 from the sheet in one line (e.g. "human common, gang outfit, ranged gun").
2. Choose the pattern from § 2. For a crew, choose the outfit word first and test it on every member.
3. Fill from § 3. Make 6 to 10 raw names; drop anything § 5 forbids.
4. If localized, run § 4.
5. Check each for sound clash with the roster, copying, existing Steam game titles, real places and organizations, plain dictionary words used as is, and unwanted meanings. Web search can't fully clear a name; write "not found", never "clear".
6. Keep 4 to 6. For each, write: the name (and localized form), pattern letter, which card-game or Lovecraft names it echoes, what it tells the player, risks, length.
7. Show them side by side and let the human pick.
