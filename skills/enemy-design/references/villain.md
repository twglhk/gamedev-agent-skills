# Villain steps: make the player know him and want him down

For a boss or mid-boss with a will. Fill the full sheet first ([sheet.md](sheet.md)), then lay these eight on top of the boss fields. Mindless monster bosses check only 4 (signature) and 6 (the fight speaks).

The aim: during play the player learns some of his story, and by the final fight badly wants to beat him. The failure it prevents is a boss with a deep story whom the player never sees, and whose deeds the player never witnesses, before the fight.

## The eight steps

1. **Want, wound, belief.** He believes he is right. He acts "because it has to be so", not "because he is evil", and has a gentle word for what he does. Moral justification and euphemistic labelling are how ordinary people allow themselves harm (Bandura).
2. **Mirror.** He wants what one of your heroes wants, or carries the same wound, and crosses the line that hero won't. One scene, in or around the fight, has him speak to that hero. The opponent fights for the same goal and attacks the hero's weakness (Truby); the protagonist is only as interesting as the forces against him (McKee).
3. **Cruelty seen.** Before the fight, in front of the player, he takes a person or thing the player has come to care for. Hatred and "I want it back" start here (Handsome Jack becomes a real threat when he kills Bloodwing, a companion's bird).
4. **Signature.** One sound, shape or verbal habit that says he is near, repeated across the run. His weight should press down even when his body is absent (Cassandra Nova's hand inside heads).
5. **Reveal in fragments.** Never tell the story at once. One fragment per place (a person's words, an object, a trace), and the last piece clicks inside the fight (Dark Souls item descriptions).
6. **The fight speaks for him.** The gimmick is his personality; the phases are the order in which his mind breaks. In an auto-battler, show it where everyone is watching.
7. **Repeats move the story on.** Beaten and back again, he remembers the team and says something new. In a roguelike, even a lost run earns one fragment (Hades: dialogue advances with each death).
8. **The fall.** He collapses by his own logic. Offer him one way out and let him refuse it; pity him if you like, but never lighten what he did.

## Three checks before art

Any blank stops the hand-off.

- Can the player name one thing he took from them? (3)
- Did the player meet his signature more than three times before the fight? (4)
- What is the last fragment, the one that only clicks in the fight? (5)

## Who writes what

Steps 3, 4, 5 and 7 are scenes placed in levels and story. The enemy designer writes the proposal (which scene, what goes there); the level and story owners place it. Don't change a hero to fit the villain; find the mirror in the heroes you have.
